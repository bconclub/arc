# 02 · References: how we read them and what we've learned

The founder sends reels while scrolling (to Insta Daemon). Our job is to **understand** each one, not to copy it.

## The breakdown (do this for every reference)
1. **Pull it**: video + info JSON (caption, likes, comments, date, size). If Instagram blocks a logged-out pull,
   open it in the logged-in BCON Chrome. See 06-tools.md.
2. **See it**: a contact sheet (24 frames across the length) and the cut list (scene changes).
3. **Hear it**: the sound's name + IG audio link; measured BPM, loudness per second, biggest rises, cuts on the beat.
   Log it in EDITR `content/social/AUDIO-LIBRARY.md`.
4. **Write it down**: hook (what's in second 1), structure by time, text on screen, look (camera, light, grade),
   format (9:16 or landscape with letterbox), CTA, length, numbers, **why it works**, **how we'd make it** (which
   tools, rough cost), **fit for which brands**.
5. **File it** in EDITR `content/social/REFERENCE-REELS.md` and its brand-type index.

## Turning a reference into our idea (founder, 2026-10-11: "you have to think like that")
1. Name the reference's **product truth**: Tom Ford "Lost Cherry" presses cherry, almond and rose into the bottle
   because a perfume *is* its notes.
2. Name the **mechanic** that shows it: a machine turns the real ingredients into the product in one move.
3. Find **our product's own truth**: what is it really made of, how is it really made?
4. Rebuild the mechanic around our truth: a hair oil gets a cold press with real herbs and golden oil flowing into
   the bottle, not a perfume compressor.
5. Write that reasoning into the brief and every prompt.

## One to infinity, not one to one
- Keep a reference's mechanics (camera, light, moves, rhythm, structure). Change the set, product and story to ours.
- A colour change is a proposal, not a default: the founder rejected teal on the cheesecake reel and kept red.
  Offer colour options; the founder picks.
- When we flip a trend, laugh at it from our angle instead of copying it ("Bangalore did it first": a Bangalore
  auto does the Roadster's flip before Tesla).
- Recreating a reel as-is (when asked) = same beats, pacing, camera, sound. Don't turn it into a product showcase.

## What makes a reference work (patterns seen again and again)
- **The payoff is the hook.** The best moment is on screen in second 1; no intro, no logo reveal.
- **Proof, not claims.** AI-ad tutorials show the prompt and inputs next to the result.
- **Comment-keyword CTA** ("comment TESLA for the prompt"): on trend accounts comments outnumber likes; the keyword
  drives reach and the DM turns viewers into followers. The promised file must exist before posting.
- **Local truth beats spectacle.** "Tesla in India" (8.8K likes in hours): the product everyone wants + a place
  everyone knows + a joke only locals get (autos, traffic, overhead wires). The same trend without a local angle or
  a face did 7x worse.
- **One annoyance, beaten fast.** "Toll booths are optional" (8.5K in 2h): the payoff in under 6s, the caption is the
  joke and ends on a question that pulls comments.
- **Music drops on the stunt.** Hold the bed down through the setup, drop it exactly on the action (+9 dB at 5.5s).
- **One point per reel.** The Claude Code mods explainer (one topic, real screens) beat every list reel we tried.
- **Short loops.** 8-15s single shots (the burger Roadster stunt, 9.6s, one continuous move) rewatch well.
- **Saturation check.** If two of three brand versions of a trend are the same template (Roadster delivers to a
  window), skip that template.

## Client briefs from references (example: Team Mithai, 22 references)
Group the client's references by what they're asking for (brand film, premium heritage, food close-ups, AI spec
ads), connect each to a brand truth from their own site, then write ideas as six-frame shot lists in the storyboard
standard. Their site imagery may be AI renders: those are not proof of the product. Ask for real photos first.
