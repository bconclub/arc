# Video craft: what makes a reel work (thread "Social Editr", 2026-10-25)

## Hook and story
- **The payoff is the hook, in the first second.** Open on the best moment (the ad itself, the crash frozen mid-air, the
  product at its peak) plus a short title. Never open on an intro, a logo build, or an address to the buyer.
- **One point per reel** (Z, 2026-10-11). The Claude mods reel (one topic, real screens) got 266/201 views on trials while
  list reels got 4-38. A "22 AIs you can text" list reel was stopped.
- **Hook = a change of visuals, not just words.** A/B trial variants must look different from frame one.
- **Motion every second.** No reciting over a still card; every spoken idea gets its own moving scene. Photo scenes
  must move (slow zooms on stills were rejected).
- **Show, don't tell.** Two text moments per piece at most (the hook and the end card); no caption narrating what the
  picture already shows.
- Short loops win (10-15s): rewatches count. A 43s single-idea explainer (frwdslash Sarvam reel, 16 likes) was too long
  and had no hook frame.

## How to think about a reference (Z: "you have to think like that")
1. Name the **product truth** the reference shows (a perfume *is* its notes, so pressing cherry and rose into the bottle).
2. Name the **mechanic** that shows it (a press that turns real ingredients into the product in one move).
3. Find **our** product's own truth (hair oil: herbs cold-pressed into oil).
4. Rebuild the mechanic around our truth, our ingredients, our palette. Never a literal swap that makes no sense.
5. Write the reasoning into the brief and the prompt so every still carries the idea.
- **Recreations are one to infinity, not one to one**: keep the mechanics (camera, light, moves, rhythm, structure),
  change the world. But a colour change is a proposal, not a default (Z kept the cheesecake reel's red world).

## Look and layout
- **Real, not AI-looking.** "Too AI" is the light and styling, not the model: one motivated light source, real
  texture (pores, crumbs, wear), ordinary people, no glossy studio polish, no neon, no lens flares, no mist.
- **Real physics, always.** A press means it fully crushes; things fall, fly and land the way they would. Vehicles
  look exactly like the real ones (a Bajaj RE has one front wheel and two rear; Tesla's Roadster thrusters are cold
  gas: white vapour, no flame).
- **Fill the frame**, big type, little dead space; but keep key words clear of the Instagram UI (top ~14%, bottom ~35%
  for ads, right-hand button column). Nothing on faces.
- **Minimal, neat type.** Z rejected a heavy black band with a gold block for the CTA ("not like this, neat and
  minimal") and liked one serif line with the keyword in a second colour in double quotes: Comment "FOOD".

## Sound
- Every reel has music; cut on its beats (cuts land on a hit). The drop can be placed to land on the first cut.
- **Endings: no gimmicks.** Z rejected a fade-out, then a riser + whoosh stack, then a "Faahh" shout: "just end it".
  End on a beat with the music stopping cleanly.
- Starts: don't add risers or effects unless asked ("you don't have to make the start").
- When Z points to a moment in a track by time, it's the time in the video he watched, not the track.

## CTA and post
- **Comment-keyword CTA** ("Comment FOOD to get the complete setup", "Comment AI") on screen and in the caption; the
  promised thing must exist before posting. Comments push reach; the AI-creator trend runs on this.
- The ending stays in motion (Z: "we cannot just freeze there"): put the CTA over a live shot, not a frozen frame.
- Ship two variations for trial reels when the hook is uncertain; post the winner.
