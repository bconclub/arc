# 03 · Stills that look real (ChatGPT)

Base standard: [`../storyboard-prompting.md`](../storyboard-prompting.md) (blocks A format, B product truth, C look,
D cast, E shot list, F avoid). Use it for every still, even a single one (founder, 2026-10-20: "We prompt like real
world").

## The look (block C)
- "Real candid photograph, not a render", a **named camera** (iPhone 15 Pro, or 35mm Kodak Portra 400), natural
  grain or sensor noise.
- **ONE motivated light source**, said by where it comes from (window light from camera left, overcast daylight, a
  single kitchen bulb). Stacked lights (hazards + headlights + interior) read as a studio and as AI.
- True-to-life colour, real texture: pores, flyaways, creases, crumbs, dust, wear.
- Never write "cinematic", "8K", "ultra-detailed", "mist", "film still, anamorphic". Lens words steer realism.
- "Too AI" is the light and the styling, not the model. What passed for people: an unedited colleague's iPhone photo,
  flat light, slightly cool white balance, mild noise, deep focus, a little off-level, ordinary people.

## The shot (block E)
Every frame = shot size + angle + lens + action + where the light falls.
24-35mm place and scale · 50mm people at a natural distance · 85mm faces · 100mm macro product.
Leave the shot size out and the model defaults to a flat eye-level medium shot.

## Product truth (block B)
- Write geometry and real size in cm, from **real photos or the client's own words only**. A brand's AI renders are
  not proof (Mithai v1 drew kaju katli as triangles; Cravio reels showed big balls, the real product is a small oval
  coffee-bean dragée).
- No invented packaging, labels or logos. No health/eco/taste claims unless cleared.
- The product is in the frame from second one; a closed box doesn't read.

## Vehicles and anything real must be exact (founder, 2026-10-19)
"Don't mess up the vehicles' look and feel, dynamics, everything. It has to be exact."
- Spell out the real geometry: a Bangalore auto is a Bajaj RE (one front wheel, two rear wheels, yellow canvas
  canopy, green body, handlebar). A Tesla Roadster matches Tesla's official photos.
- Once a still gets it right, attach that still as the reference: "use exactly THIS auto".
- Zoom in and compare against the reference before sending. Our first hero drew the auto as a box van.

## Physics and scale
- Show what really happens, at real scale. Founder on the auto-flip: "wrong physics... We just want a little,
  little flip... a small ramp on the road." Tesla's real demo is a short, grounded flip; the viral AI versions
  (4 m flights, blue flames) are the fake ones. Cold-gas thrusters = white vapour, never fire or blue glow.
- People and loose things move with the vehicle (upside down means the driver is upside down too).
- The same product never grows or shrinks between frames. Hands: five fingers, natural grip.

## Consistency
- Same chat for the whole job; repeat B/C/D word for word; attach the approved still as the reference.
- Same person: the exact same description (hair, every garment, colours) every time; redo any drift.
- Same place and direction: subjects face the same way across keyframes or the motion reads as reversing.
- No doors (handles flip sides, people pass through them). Phones face the person reading them.
- AI food close-ups sneak in a stranger's face at the edge: crop to hands and product.
- Scan for real brand names the model sneaks in (a ZARA sign, a real mall on a receipt).

## Text in images
- Text-heavy cards (end cards, menus) can be made in ChatGPT, but check every word against the source, letter by
  letter, before use (the Calvary end card passed: menu, date, venue, BOOK NOW all exact). Leave space for the real
  logo and lay the logo file on top in the edit.
- Prompts with line breaks can half-send in ChatGPT; write the prompt as one paragraph with separators.
