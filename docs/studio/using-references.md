# Studio references: take the style, not the story

House rule for every editor and agent (EDITR Main, Social Editr, Insta Daemon, ARC Studio) who uses the reference library (ARC > Studio > References; data in `EDITR/content/proxe/fx`).

Z (17 Nov 2026): "You have seen it and you are trying to make it exactly replicating that. That's not what I would want. I would use that reference plus a new brand and details, and it should replicate to that brand. If I'm taking the crayons reference and putting it to a digital marketing company, the look and feel should be what you're extracting out of it, and the music and the way it interacts. Not the exact storybook."

## The rule

A reference gives us **how it looks, sounds and moves**. The brand gives us **what happens and what is said**. Never carry the reference's shots, objects, characters or lines into a brand's film.

The saved prompt on each reference is a remake of that one clip (its shot list is the reference's own story). Use it as the source to read the style from. Do not paste it and swap the product name.

## What to pull from a reference (the style sheet)

| Field | What it holds | Example: H0053 Crayon Kawaii Doodle |
|---|---|---|
| Idea of the look | The one visual principle, in a sentence | The real thing stays photographic; the world around it is hand-drawn |
| Surface and colour | Ground, palette, texture | Textured cream paper, soft coloured-pencil tones, no saturated digital colour |
| Drawing or type | Line, lettering, graphic language | Thin pencil outlines, flat fills, childlike doodles; no thick black outlines, no 3D |
| Light and lens | Light source, lens feel | One soft warm lamp from upper left, one glint on the subject; macro then straight-on |
| How it moves | Motion grammar, how things enter and leave | Doodles draw themselves on, burst outward, drift to the edges; subject barely moves |
| Pace | Length, number of shots, hold lengths | About 10 s, two shots: a short close opener, then one long held frame that keeps changing |
| Sound | Tempo, instruments, where hits land | No bass or drums; toy piano or glockenspiel, small two-note answers just after each visual change |
| Feeling | What the viewer feels | Gentle, storybook, playful |
| Never | The reference's own avoid list | No 3D cartoon characters, the subject never turns into a drawing |

Left behind on purpose: the toy camera, the moth, the caterpillar, the insects, the ferns. Those are the reference's story.

## What comes from the brand (the story)

Product or service, the one promise, the proof, the audience, the brand's colours, logo and words. Written from the brand's real facts (see `storyboard-prompting.md`, block B).

## How to apply it

1. Write the style sheet from the reference (table above).
2. Write the brand's story: one promise, three to six beats, from the brand's facts.
3. Ask, beat by beat: "how would this look show this beat?" and write new shots.
4. Swap the palette for the brand's where the look allows it; keep the texture, light, motion, pace and sound.
5. Write a new music prompt from the sound field, timed to the new cuts.

Worked example: H0053 for a digital marketing company. Not a product with insects around it. A real phone, photographed, on cream paper. Around it, pencil doodles draw themselves: a little megaphone, hearts and comment bubbles bursting outward, a wobbly growth arrow curling up the side, a doodled crowd walking in from the edges. Two shots, one long held frame, toy piano with two-note answers on each new doodle. Same look, feel, movement and sound. A different story.

## Check before it goes to Z

| Check | Pass when |
|---|---|
| Not a copy | No object, character, shot order or line from the reference survives, unless the brand really has it |
| Still the look | Someone who knows the reference would recognise its look, movement and sound |
| Brand true | Every claim, product shape and size is the brand's own (see `storyboard-prompting.md`) |
| Sound fits | Music follows the reference's sound field and lands on the new cuts |

## What the library holds today (audit, 17 Nov 2026)

- 7,312 references collected; 1,153 kept (rated 4 or 5): 628 video, 525 photo. Every kept one has a remake prompt and a preview.
- Look, light and lens: readable from the prompt on almost all kept videos (610 of 628 name a lens or framing; 582 describe movement; 494 name colour).
- Pace: 623 of 628 state the length; only 77 carry timecodes for each shot.
- Sound: 613 of 628 videos have measured music notes (428 with a tempo, 225 with hit times); 470 have a music prompt. The notes are measured from the audio, not listened to, so instruments are a guess.
- Lettering: only 184 of 628 say anything about type.
- Not stored anywhere yet: the style sheet as separate fields. Today the style is mixed into the remake prompt with the reference's own story.
