## 2. ICP has three layers: market, company, person
Work it backwards from a deal that closed (or is about to): who is this buyer, and where are more like him?
1. **IMP, ideal market profile.** Which markets value what we do? Learn it from who actually comes and buys.
2. **ICoP, ideal company profile.** In that market, which companies: staff size, revenue, how sales is set up,
   number of locations, lead volume.
3. **IPP, ideal prospect profile.** In those companies, which designation, and who is the person: their KPIs,
   motivators, what they like to hear and don't (psychographics).
- From Astroshift: "**single founder struggling with 100 WhatsApps a day**" is an ICP attribute. Write hypotheses
  like it and test them. People move fast when they are already losing.
- Use ICP **before list building**: decide who to talk to before what to say. Two ways to build the hypothesis:
  look at who already bought, or research who buys similar things.
- **Each offering has its own ICP.** PROXe's ICP (heavy lead flow) is not BCON's lead-gen ICP (needs leads). Different
  people, different outreach, different effort. Don't lead PROXe calls with lead gen.

## 3. Count each experiment on its own, with a deadline
- Z promised 15 demos last week and did 3, all from old leads. The coach: from the new experiment that is **zero**.
  "Three is just to make me feel better." Counting old-lead demos as new-experiment results hides whether the new
  ICP works.
- Tag every lead and demo with the experiment it came from (new ICP outbound, new ads, old-pool rework).
- Set a deadline: how many leads, how many demos, by what date. If two weeks of the new work brings no leads, that is
  the red flag: no sustained contact with reality.
- **Targets from 17 Nov:** each caller makes **20 good calls a day** (not 100) and books **2 demos a day**; **15 demos
  per person a week**; **30 demos by Wednesday 25 Nov** across the two callers. Today's Astroshift demo is the first.
- The one thing to watch: is the new ICP bringing demos? That is the only result that says it works.

## 4. Don't hire more until the system holds
Z wanted to add two more callers now. Coach: no. Run these two for 15 days to a month and build the structure first
(how to build the list, how to call, the scripts), so it doesn't depend on Z or on these two. The next two should get
up to speed in half the time. More people before structure gives you more reasons things aren't working, not
results.

## 5. Z's caller system (worth selling)
Z built a dialer: callers ring from it, PROXe's lead data shows on screen, the qualifying questions tick off as they
are asked, and at the end the system marks ICP or not and pushes it to Z. Coach: package it and sell it; every PROXe
customer gets it (team management, lead dialer). "The best way to build a product is to use it yourself."

## 6. Qualify before any demo: Z's four questions
No demo unless these are answered on the call:
1. How many leads do you get (a day)?
2. Are you running ads?
3. Do you have a team handling them?
4. Are you having trouble with this?
Then company profiling: do they have a good website, are they placed well, would their logo on our site mean
something, are they on our Dream 100? Answer these from the first call. Prepare the Dream 100 list and keep it.
After that, the session 09 rule: get their loss in their numbers before the demo.

## 7. On the call: check understanding without annoying them
Z stops every 5 to 10 minutes: "Are you with me? Are you following?" Coach: careful, past a point it flips and works
against you even if they understand. Read them instead: eye contact, face, body language (micro-expressions), and
ask small indirect questions rather than "are you ready to pay?" Watch their face when money comes up. Get to what
they are not saying.

## 8. Reviews and testimonials: do the writing for them
(From the Mind and Matter review discussion; applies to PROXe case studies.)
- Busy people never "find time" to write a review. Goodwill runs out in a day or two.
- Call them, have a short feedback conversation, then draft the review from their own words and send it: "Approve
  this?" One minute of their time. Use that: "You don't have one minute?"
- Our rule: ask before recording, and get approval before publishing a name or quote.
- Best is a short video testimonial on a call. Collect them fast, while the result is fresh.
- Decision-maker reviews (owner, manager) sell to the next buyer; end-user reviews build engagement. Keep both, use
  them differently.

## 9. Email
Use an open tracker on outreach emails, so we know who opens before deciding the next step.

5. Case studies by the draft-and-approve method.
