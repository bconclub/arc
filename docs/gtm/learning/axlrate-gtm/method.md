> **Date:** 2026-10-22 (sessions 22 Sep to 22 Oct)  
> **From:** Axlrate sales cohort: Z's handwritten notes and the 22 Oct call transcript  
> **Use it:** Every GTM agent, before writing any outbound line, call script or plan


Source: Axlrate cohort sessions, Z's handwritten notes (00 to 07, 02 missing) and the 22 Oct 2026 call transcript.
The Google Sheet "PROXe GTM Trifecta" (tabs Trifecta, Causal Law, PROXe GTM, TEMQ) holds the worked versions.

## The models
- **Trifecta.** Buyer stages (awareness, consideration, purchase) mapped to funnel (TOFU, MOFU, BOFU) and lead stages
  (gen, management, closing), each with activities, tools, process and "company's ability" scored 1 to 5. Every
  activity needs a routine so it actually gets done. Compare perceived ability with what is actually done.
- **Causal Law.** 1 Sustained contact with reality (be in the buyer's world), 2 Cognition, 3 Structure, 4 Clarity,
  5 Alignment, 6 Integration, 7 Outcomes. These run together, not step by step.
- **TEMQ.** Time, Effort, Money, Quality. Score what the buyer SAYS (words) and what they DO (actions), each 1 to 5.
  Actions count; words mostly don't. The gap is the read.
- **VPP.** The buyer perceives you as a Value provider, then a Problem solver, then a Painkiller. Everyone starts as a
  value provider. The difference between the levels is urgency. Buying is easy only at painkiller. Pain is real when
  lost time, effort, money or quality can be put in numbers (VPP and TEMQ together). Not seen as a painkiller means
  not aligned yet.
- **Three positionings:** the person (Z), the offering (PROXe), the company. Buyers bet on the person.

## Lines that guide every agent
- There is no selling, only buying. Sales is an outcome of alignment.
- Getting aligned is the only KPI.
- Don't talk about the product. Serve, then they buy. Your effort doesn't matter unless it moves the buyer.
- They will never tell you what they want. Notice, and ask questions from the outcome.
- No convincing, no "approach". Ask what they want, what they require, what is critical.
- Slow down to speed up. Stay in the breakdown. Create the commitment point.
- Trust comes from going deep into their problem (like a doctor), not from more features.
- Stalling ("after Diwali") means they see you as a value provider. Name it: "You said these problems go away with
  this, but you want to wait two months. So you're OK living with them?"
- Money is about managing THEIR commitment, not your emotion. Full payment upfront.
- Every conversation has one outcome set before it starts. Move with the buyer; no chasing, no pushing.
- Money (Actions) counts only what moves money towards us (finance team, vendor registration, invoice, payment).
  Agreeing to the price is words; their spend elsewhere is capability, not commitment.
- Pain = problem x TEMQ impact, in rupees, worked out with them. Then leave them unsettled.
- Free work only after they feel the pain and see the value, and tied to payment.
- This is not about you. Be curiously engaged in how they perceive things, like a journalist.
