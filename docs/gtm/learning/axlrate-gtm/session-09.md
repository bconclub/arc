## 2. Loss as per TEMQ, in the first conversation
The core of the session. Before any demo, trial or proposal, find out what the current way is costing **them**:
- **Time:** how much time is lost because of how it runs today?
- **Effort:** how much effort is wasted?
- **Money:** how much money goes down the drain?
- **Quality:** what quality are they losing?
Write their numbers down and keep them in the account note. This is their loss, not ours. "If you don't know their
loss, very quickly it becomes your loss": you start spending to fix their problem for free.
For PROXe this is the session 08 pain line (leads a day x share missed or late x close rate x deal value) plus time
and effort (front-desk hours on calls, follow-ups nobody does) and quality (leads that go cold, bad reviews).
Without the loss, every demo is feature-selling: "This is the feature, please buy. That is not selling, that is
begging."

## 3. Trials: check on day 2, trigger on their loss
- A trial is a commitment test, not a gift. Watch Time and Effort **in their actions**: are they logging in, using it,
  getting their team on it?
- **On day 2, not day 7 or 15:** if nobody has used it, call and say it straight, using the loss they gave you:
  "You said you were losing X because of this, so I started the trial. Two days, nobody has logged in. Is this still a
  problem? The trial costs me every day. Shall I keep it on or switch it off and give it to someone else?"
- Reminding them to use it is convincing. Naming their own loss is triggering. If they say "we'll start today", hold
  it on; if they drop it, the problem was not a problem and you saved two weeks.
- Ghosting after a demo or trial means we never got a grip on the loss at the start. Don't chase it later; hit it at
  the beginning.
- Free credits are still money. Count trial cost as real.
- Z's practice the coach backed: a WhatsApp group per trial, and when they hit a problem Z gets the team on a call
  and walks them through the dashboard, rather than sending texts or videos. Z is the catalyst for the change.

## 4. Fear of replacement is real resistance
Example from Z: a hospital's management gave us the front-desk contact, and the front desk will not pick up because
AI puts their job at stake. In a business with many staff, each person's insecurity stacks up. Resistance shows as
silence, not as a "no". Treat it as a knowledge gap: educate the staff on what changes for them (PROXe takes the
repetitive replies, they handle the patients who arrive), from the space of helping them perform, not of winning the
deal. Get management's loss first, then position the staff as the people who gain.

## 5. Results are always a delta on their loss
Mind and Matter's example: a school had 100% pass after their programme. Worth little alone. Ask what it was the
three years before (say 85%, 65%, 85%): "Your teachers put in the time and effort, and you got about 85%. We came in
and closed the 15%." Then take that story to similar buyers: "This school averaged 85% for three years; with us it
reached 100%. Do you want that?"
For PROXe case studies (Zensquare, Scan2Kare, Ahoy, Windchasers): always before vs after, in their numbers (missed
leads, reply time, bookings per week before PROXe and after). A result with no baseline is not a result.

## 6. Words are cheap; only what you can see, you can move
"I'll sign up, we'll onboard in December" means nothing until it shows in Time, Effort, Money and Quality actions.
"Only what you can see, you can move." If you can't see it, intervene and change course.
