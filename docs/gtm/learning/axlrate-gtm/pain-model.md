# PROXe sales pain model (VPP + TEMQ loss), for agents and the voice agent

> **Updated:** 2026-11-12, from Axlrate sessions 07, 08, 09  
> **Lives in:** ARC Supabase `arc_context`: `sales_pain_model` (this), `gtm_learnings` (method + session notes),
> `gtm_handoff` (tasks for the GTM agent), `gtm_brief` (offer, rules). Pull from there.  
> **Use it:** the outbound voice agent, call-prep agents, follow-up writers, trial watchers, case-study writers.  
> **Keep inside:** use it in our calls and messages. Never name the framework (TEMQ, VPP, "loss as per TEMQ") to a
> prospect, and never post or share it outside our team and Axlrate cohort 24.

## 1. The idea in one line
Nobody buys a feature. They buy when they feel what the current way is costing them. Find that cost, in their
numbers, before any demo. No loss, no demo.

## 2. How the buyer sees us (VPP)
- **Value provider:** "nice to have". They listen, ask little, say "after Diwali", "send details". No urgency.
- **Problem solver:** "this could fix X". Some questions, some interest.
- **Painkiller:** "we are losing money every day, how fast can this start?" Buying is easy only here.
Everyone starts as value provider. What moves them up is their own loss, said out loud, in rupees. A move that does
not raise their engagement means the lead is not good; stop spending time on it.

## 3. Commitment (TEMQ): watch actions, not words
Time, Effort, Money, Quality, each scored on Words (what they say) and Actions (what they do). Words are cheap.
- Time/Effort actions: they give a second call, share numbers, join a group, bring their team, use the trial.
- Money actions, only these count: loop in finance, ask for GST or vendor registration, ask for an invoice or bank
  details, acknowledge an invoice with a pay date, pay. Agreeing to the price is words. Their ad spend elsewhere is
  capability, not commitment. Asking for a proposal is Time/Effort.
- Default Money (Actions) = 1 until one of those happens.

## 4. Loss as per TEMQ: the questions (PROXe)
Ask in the first conversation, write every number down. It is their loss, never ours.
- **Leads:** How many enquiries do you get a day (calls, WhatsApp, Instagram, website)? From ads or walk-ins?
- **Missed / late (Time):** How many go unanswered or get a reply after an hour? What happens after 8 pm and on
  Sundays? How long does a reply usually take?
- **Effort:** Who answers them today? How many hours a day does that person spend on calls and WhatsApp? Who does the
  follow-ups, and how many actually get done?
- **Money:** Out of 10 leads, how many become customers? What is one customer worth to you (first bill, or a year)?
  How much do you spend on ads a month?
- **Quality:** Do leads go cold or go to a competitor? Do you lose track of who said "call me later"? Any complaints
  about slow replies?
- **Then make it one number, with them:** leads a day x share missed or late x close rate x value per customer =
  rupees lost a day, then a month. Example (Genie Bikez): 80 to 100 leads a day, 10 to 20 good, ~Rs 2,000 margin a
  bike, so ~Rs 40,000 a day at stake. "You are losing about 40K a day. PROXe is 10K a month."
- **Then ask how they feel about it** ("How are you feeling about this number?") and **leave them unsettled.** Do not
  pitch in the same breath. "Let me think about your setup and come back." If they chase us, the pain landed.

## 5. Call flow for the outbound voice agent
1. **One outcome per call, set before dialing.** For a new or warm lead the outcome is their loss numbers and a
   second call with Z. Not a demo, not a price, not a payment link.
2. **Open on them, not PROXe.** Reason for the call in one line, tied to their business ("we work with clinics in
   Bangalore on missed enquiries; quick question about how you handle yours").
3. **Ask the section 4 questions,** a few at a time, in their language (English, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam as they
   speak). Listen more than talk. Repeat their numbers back to confirm.
4. **Say the loss back in rupees, using only their numbers.** Never invent or round up a number they did not give.
   If they don't know a number, ask for a rough guess; if still none, log "unknown" and move on.
5. **Check reality.** If they are engaged (asking questions, giving numbers, giving time), propose the next step:
   "Our founder can look at your setup and show what changes. Tomorrow 11 or 4?" If they are not engaged, reset to a
   smaller outcome (one number, or permission to WhatsApp) and end politely.
6. **Never:** push a demo before the loss is known, offer anything free, send a payment link, promise results or
   guarantees, or argue. If asked "can you guarantee it?": we guarantee every lead is answered, day and night; their
   team still has to close.
7. **Log after every call:** each loss number (leads/day, share missed, reply time, hours spent, close rate, value per
   customer, ad spend), the rupee loss said back, VPP stage (value provider / problem solver / painkiller), TEMQ
   actions seen, the outcome reached, the next step and time.

## 6. Trials: day-2 check
- Day 2, not day 7. If they have not used it (no conversations, no logins), Z calls with their own loss: "You said
  you were losing X a day, so we started the trial. Two days, nobody has used it. Is this still a problem? The trial
  costs us daily. Keep it on, or switch it off and give it to someone else?"
- Reminding is convincing. Naming their own loss is triggering. Ghosting means the loss was never gripped at the start.
- Fear of replacement (front desk not picking up because AI may take their job) is silent resistance. Get
  management's loss first, then show staff what they gain: PROXe takes the repetitive replies, they handle the people
  who come in.

## 7. Cold or silent leads
Outcome is a reply, not a meeting. Reread the last conversation, find their pain, send one line in their numbers:
"I was working on this and thought of you..." / "If this is still bothering you, let's talk." No "hi, how are you".
No grip on their pain, no message.

## 8. Results and case studies
Always a delta on their loss: before PROXe vs after, in their numbers (missed leads, reply time, bookings a week). A
result without a baseline is not a result. Then take the story to similar buyers: "This clinic missed ~30% of
enquiries; now every one gets a reply in under a minute. Do you want that?"

## 9. Free work and payment
Free help only after they feel the pain and see the value, and tied to payment ("If you go ahead, I'll take care of
the site"). Never a payment link before alignment. Full payment upfront.
